What we heard

Practical AI to run the clinic as a business

Premium ophthalmology clinics in the MEDCEE region need practical AI skills to attract patients, communicate with them, educate them and support them. The program serves that need with three pillars, and its design follows four rules from our conversation.

01

Tools for content

Create patient education and marketing content faster and at a higher standard.

02

Tools for patient support

Improve interaction, engagement and communication along the whole patient journey.

03

Best practices

Real examples from other clinics and healthcare teams: a reality attendees can copy.

A series, not an event

Skills need practice and repetition, so the program runs over several weeks.

Hands-on every time

Attendees bring a laptop and build their own examples in every session.

Blended

In-person workshops for the practical work, short online sessions in between.

For the whole team

Doctors, managers, nurses, optometrists, front desk and marketing.

The series

Five connected sessions

Each session builds on the previous one and uses a different part of the patient journey as its example. Between sessions, each clinic completes a short assignment on its own real material.

Show my path

Every session is open to the whole team. Pick a role to see which sessions matter most to it.

In-person workshop · 3 hours Online session · 75–90 min 30-minute assignment between sessions

Each session starts with a review of two or three assignments from the previous one. That review is where the repetition happens.

The sessions are modular: each one works with or without the others, and they fit inside a wider program. If travel allows only one in-person event, sessions 2 and 5 merge into one full day and the rest stay online.

Who it's for

Mixed teams from premium clinics

No technical background is needed. Two or three people per clinic work better than one, because they apply the tools together afterwards.

Surgeons and doctors

Faster preparation of patient explanations, talks and training material. A method to keep up with the literature.

Clinic managers and owners

A view of where AI saves time and wins patients. A short action plan for the clinic.

Nurses, optometrists and patient counsellors

Clear pre-op and post-op instructions, answers to frequent questions, material in the patient's language.

Front desk and marketing staff

Templates for messages, reminders, review replies, web and social content.

Best practices and real cases

People adopt what they see working

Every session includes at least one real case from clinics, hospital teams and medical societies, inside and outside ophthalmology. Each case follows the same short format.

Before

The situation

The task, who did it and how long it took.

Change

What the team changed

The tool and the exact way they use it.

After

The result

What improved, and what they would do differently.

From session 3, the best assignments from the group become cases too. By session 5 the group has its own examples from its own region.

Format and logistics

How it runs

How the series runs in practice. Everything here can be adjusted to the wider program.

Language

English. Attendees work in their own language during the exercises: the tools handle all of them.

Location

In-person workshops in Prague or another city of the region, wherever suits the group best.

Rhythm

One session every two to three weeks. The full series takes about 12 weeks.

Tools

General AI assistants (such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini) and a small set of tools for documents, visuals and video. Free versions are enough for most exercises.

Materials

A prompt library, the templates built in each session and the recordings of the online sessions.

Patient data

No exercise uses identifiable patient data.

Equipment

One laptop per attendee, with internet access. A room with tables for group work and a projector.

No experience needed

No coding, no prior use of AI. Each session starts from the basics it needs.

Real clinic material

Attendees work on their own leaflets, frequent questions and weekly messages, never on identifiable patient data.
